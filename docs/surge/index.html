Does the wiring matter?

Three flies start the same distance from a smell, with the same number of connections, the same connection counts, and the same rule for what to do. Only one thing differs between them: which neuron is wired to which. Nothing here was trained.

What you are watching

The fly is a real one: the MaleCNS v1.0 connectome of a single male Drosophila, 166,700 neurons, run as a rate model. Odour concentration at its two antennae drives the olfactory receptor neurons. One step of network dynamics runs per frame, carrying its own state forward. The turn is read from lateral horn output: left side minus right side.

The shuffled fly is the same brain with its wiring permuted — every neuron keeps its number of inputs and outputs and every synapse keeps its count, but who connects to whom is scrambled. It gets the identical controller, the identical odours, the identical starting points.

The steering sign was measured, not chosen. On odours and receptor neurons it had never seen, the real wiring says which antenna was stimulated more with 0.715 accuracy against ten shuffled controls spanning 0.441 to 0.620. That measurement is what steers the fly here.

Every track drawn is one of the scored episodes — same seed, same batch, same condition. None of them was picked for looking good.

What this is not

  • This is not how a fly navigates. A real fly does not steer on smell directly — odour updates a goal direction, and steering comes from comparing that goal against an internal compass in the central complex. None of that is here. What you are watching is closer to a bacterium following a gradient.
  • Smooth gradients only. Walking flies in turbulent plumes navigate by the timing of odour encounters, not by the difference between their antennae. This says nothing about that case.
  • Excitatory and inhibitory signs are predicted, not measured. They come from neurotransmitter identity: glutamate is treated as inhibitory, and 3,177 neurons with no clear call default to excitatory. Both arms carry the same assumption, so the comparison holds — but no claim about absolute circuit function does.
  • There is no body. No legs, no ventral nerve cord, no inertia. The turn is read from a sensory area, not a motor one.
  • “Turn toward the stronger side” is an assumption about attraction, not something the wiring supplied. Every arm gets it, including the shuffled ones.
  • The statistics are weak. Five shuffled seeds give a one-sided p of about 1/6. The margin is wide; the number of seeds is not.
